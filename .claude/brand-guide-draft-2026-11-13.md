## 1. One-liner

We help local service business owners connect with the people they can help the most, so the business they spent 10, 15, 20 years building stays local.

## 2. Story

Brittany was a social worker who answered a Craigslist ad for a free-rent site manager job. Over coffee she talked the owner into letting her run all 55 of his units, did everything wrong at first, and figured it out with a pro bono lawyer and a PhD from YouTube University. That became Sagareus Property Management: 850 units in about 6 years (Confirm), which she still owns. After years of quietly helping friends and family grow their service businesses, she started Unbound in 2026 to do it on purpose, with a handful of owner-operators she picks. It isn't about the money. Local businesses are the backbone of the economy and they're becoming an endangered species, and Unbound exists to help them stay strong enough to sell or hand down to someone in their own community.

## 3. Who we serve

**Best fit:** owner-operators of local service businesses, a year or more in, who already do good work and want their brand to match the service they give customers, employees and their community. Usually plumbers, roofers, electricians and the like (the offer is built for about $500K to $1M a year; Confirm).

**Best customer story:** Gap (skipped).

**Who we don't serve:**
- Businesses under a year old that are still figuring out what they do. "I'm not a therapist. I can't help someone figure out what they're trying to do. I can help them take what they're doing and make it better."
- Owners who only care about maximizing profit, with no commitment to quality work, decent jobs, or being a good neighbor.
- Anyone who is blatantly disrespectful to us or our team.

**What they worry about before hiring us:**
- Wasting money or getting scammed.
- Getting handed more to do, not less.
- Not knowing what they don't know, which makes paying for it scary.
- Not being able to measure it ("how many leads did YouTube get us?").
- Results that aren't immediate.

**Their words:** scammed, wasting money, more on my plate, how do I know it's working.

## 4. Values

All five weigh the same.

**Simplicity.** Fewer things, done right, for the right people.
Story: Sagareus used to take any property and any owner. One week in 2016 brought in a fourplex in Tacoma, a luxury rental in Bellevue, a small office building and an industrial boat lift. Different owners, tenants and vendors, different expectations, and eventually 15 different procedure sets. "Chaos ensues." The more we narrowed down to one kind of client, the easier it got to set expectations, meet them, and hire the right staff and vendors.

**Resourcefulness.** Figure it out with what you have.
Story: websites, email campaigns, follow-ups, contracts patched together from other people's agreements. A PhD from YouTube University. "Being a business owner is basically resourcefulness." Doing it yourself first means that when you do hire someone, you understand and value what they do.

**Freedom.** Getting to choose.
"Business owners hate working 9 to 5 so much they trade it to work 5 to 9." Freedom means choosing what you do and what your company won't do, and it comes with the responsibility. "I could make way more money with less freedom, but I don't want to." If it costs your peace, it's too expensive. Success is liking yourself, liking what you do, and liking how you do it (Maya Angelou).

**Ownership.** Owning outcomes, actions, decisions and shortcomings, in business and in life. Always look for your part, even when it wasn't your fault.
Story: Gap (no specific story yet).

**Fun.** Make the boring stuff fun.
Story: at Sagareus, SOPs reach the team from Bruce, the No Drama Llama, instead of from the boss.

**What we will never do:** sell someone something that won't help solve their problem.

**Who we hire:** positive attitude, problem solvers, resourceful people who own their mistakes and learn from them. An explanation is not an excuse.
**What we'd fire for:** constant excuses, blaming others, not being able to figure things out.

## 5. Stances

Ranked by how strongly Brittany agrees (0 to 10). Used for social posts and content, not on the website (Brittany, 2026-11-13).

### 5.1 Everything works. Do less, longer. (10)
- **The issue:** owners think they have to do everything, or that some channel "doesn't work".
- **What most companies say:** be everywhere, try every channel.
- **Our stance:** "Doing 10 things at 50% is still an F across the board. You're better off doing one thing at 100%. Ideally, three things at 100%."
- **Why:** every channel works if you pick the right ones for you and keep at it until it does.
- **How it shows up:** Local + Loud is a short list of fundamentals done consistently, not a menu.
- **Where it stops:** we never promise a channel works overnight.

### 5.2 Give 10 to get 1. (10)
- **The issue:** businesses guard what they know and only give value after the sale.
- **What most companies say:** don't give away the secret sauce.
- **Our stance:** "If you have a service heart, a giving heart, it comes back to you in ways you wouldn't expect."
- **Why:** teaching freely builds trust, and the right customers self-select in.
- **How it shows up:** free tools, free lessons, free guides (this intake is one of them).
- **Where it stops:** Gap.

### 5.3 Reviews are a fundamental. (10)
- **The issue:** reviews get asked for when someone remembers.
- **What most companies say:** spend on ads.
- **Our stance:** "Feedback and reviews are a fundamental." Ask for feedback first, then ask happy customers for a review.
- **Why:** for trades, trust decides the call, and reviews are where trust gets checked.
- **How it shows up:** every client gets a feedback-then-review pipeline.
- **Where it stops:** Gap. (Proposed: we never gate, fake or buy reviews.)

### 5.4 Start SEO now. It's like exercise. (10)
- **The issue:** owners wait to do SEO until they "need" it.
- **What most companies say:** run ads for fast results.
- **Our stance:** start building SEO immediately, because it takes a long time. Stay consistent with it, like exercise.
- **Why:** the business that started a year earlier is the one that shows up first.
- **How it shows up:** SEO starts in month one of every engagement.
- **Where it stops:** Gap. (Proposed: we never promise rankings or a date.)

### 5.5 Your personality is the point. (10)
- **The issue:** productized agencies make every local business look the same.
- **What most companies say:** our proven template works for everyone.
- **Our stance:** one-size-fits-all "gets vanilla, and it loses the personality of the business itself."
- **Why:** your voice, your look and your opinions are what make you different from the company down the street.
- **How it shows up:** the deliverables are a package, but the brand, voice, visuals and stances are custom and baked into every campaign. No two clients look the same.
- **Where it stops:** packaging isn't bad. It helps at a certain stage.

### 5.6 Keep local businesses local. (8)
- **The issue:** private equity is buying up local service businesses.
- **What most companies say:** nothing, or "build to sell to the highest bidder".
- **Our stance:** "Nothing against the guys. It's the overall trend." Roll-ups that buy four plumbing companies in one town and keep all four brands, so customers get three quotes from what is really one company, aren't great. Neither is running at a loss until the competition is gone, then raising prices.
- **Why:** owners who built something over 10 to 20 years should be able to hand it to someone in their community.
- **How it shows up:** we build the brand, systems and processes that make a business worth more and easier to hand down.
- **Where it stops:** we never attack private equity people, and we never say "never sell". If you do sell to them, strong branding, systems and clean books get you a higher valuation.

### 5.7 You can't out-market a bad service. (8)
- **The issue:** high-pressure marketing of mediocre work.
- **What most companies say:** we can sell anything.
- **Our stance:** "You'll never out-market a poor product. Eventually, people will find out." Marketing and sales should match the quality of the work.
- **Why:** pushing a bad service hard is unethical, and it catches up with you.
- **How it shows up:** we only take clients whose work is already good.
- **Where it stops:** Gap.

### 5.8 Social builds trust. Leads take intent. (7)
- **The issue:** social media is sold to trades as a lead source.
- **What most companies say:** post daily and the leads will come.
- **Our stance:** for a plumber, roofer or electrician, people find you on Google or by referral, then check your social to see that you're real. "Just posting is just posting." Social can bring leads, but only when you decide to make it a lead source and are intentional about it.
- **Why:** the buyer's biggest question is "is this person trustworthy, or am I going to get scammed?"
- **How it shows up:** consistent, real posts built for trust. Lead campaigns only when they're designed as lead campaigns.
- **Where it stops:** we never say social media doesn't work.

### 5.9 Be as open about price as you can. (7)
- **The issue:** prices hidden until the estimate.
- **What most companies say:** every job is different, call for a quote.
- **Our stance:** be as transparent as possible. When an exact price is impossible, give an instant-quote range on your website so people know roughly what it costs before you go out.
- **Why:** people self-select in or out, which saves everyone time.
- **How it shows up:** instant-estimate widgets and published pricing (Unbound's own price is public).
- **Where it stops:** we don't force exact prices where the job really varies.

### 5.10 Work with the tools you already use. (7)
- **The issue:** agencies make owners switch software to suit the agency.
- **What most companies say:** move to our platform.
- **Our stance:** minimize change. "We build it into their existing system, so they don't have to switch from Housecall Pro."
- **Why:** owners hire help to take things off their plate, not to add a migration.
- **How it shows up:** custom integrations that fit the client's existing workflow.
- **Where it stops:** sometimes change is unavoidable, and we'll say so.

## 6. Voice

**Traits (this, not that):**
- **Plain, not polished.** "Fisher-Price simple", not "a holistic framework".
- **Confident, not humble.** State what works. No "we're still learning".
- **Cheeky, not jokey.** A smirk, not a stand-up set: "Doing 10 things at 50% is still an F."
- **Real, not perfect.** Own the mess: "I did everything wrong", "broke a few laws in the process", "long story short".
- **Kind about people, blunt about trends.** "Nothing against the guys. It's the trend."

**Format:** a short, punchy line up top, then the real story underneath.

**Words and lines we use:** long story short, figure it out, fundamentals, Fisher-Price simple, PhD from YouTube University, if it costs your peace it's too expensive, give 10 to get 1, just posting is just posting, what problem are we solving?

**Words we never use:** synergy, holistic, seamless, thought leadership, disruptive innovation. No em dashes.

**Primary quote (Brittany, 2026-11-13):** "Local businesses are the backbone of the economy, and they're becoming an endangered species."

**Who we sound like:** Brittany. On the phone: "Hi, this is B."

**Sample caption:**
> Ask for feedback before you ask for a review.
>
> When you ask how the job went first, you find out what to fix while you can still fix it. Happy customers get the review link. Everyone else gets a call. Simple, and it's how you get to 5 stars and stay there.

**Sample website headline:**
> Doing 10 things at 50% is still an F.
> We help local businesses do the three that matter, really well.
